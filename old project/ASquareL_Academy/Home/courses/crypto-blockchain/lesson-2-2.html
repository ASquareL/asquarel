Lesson 2.2: Crypto Exchanges

Duration: 55 minutes | Difficulty: Beginner

What is a Crypto Exchange?

A cryptocurrency exchange is a platform where you can buy, sell, and trade cryptocurrencies. There are two main types: centralized (CEX) and decentralized (DEX).

Centralized Exchanges (CEX)

Centralized exchanges are managed by a company that holds custody of your assets and operates the trading platform.

How CEX Works

  1. You deposit funds (fiat or crypto)
  2. Exchange holds your funds in their wallets
  3. You trade through their orderbook
  4. You withdraw to your personal wallet

Advantages of CEX

  • User-friendly interface
  • Fast transactions
  • High liquidity
  • Fiat onramp/offramp (buy with credit card)
  • Customer support
  • Advanced trading features

Disadvantages of CEX

  • Counterparty risk (exchange bankruptcy, hacking)
  • KYC requirements (privacy concerns)
  • Regulatory restrictions by jurisdiction
  • Trading fees
  • Not truly decentralized (violates crypto principles)

Popular CEX Platforms

  • Coinbase: Beginner-friendly, US-based
  • Kraken: Good fees, security focused
  • Binance: Largest exchange, most trading pairs
  • Gemini: Regulated, premium offering
  • FTX/Bybit: Derivatives and futures trading

Decentralized Exchanges (DEX)

DEX are blockchain-based platforms where you trade directly from your wallet without needing to deposit funds to a central authority.

How DEX Works

  1. Connect your wallet to the DEX
  2. You remain in control of your private keys
  3. Trades happen directly between wallets
  4. Funds go straight to your wallet
Key Difference: With DEX, you never give custody of your funds to anyone. You trade directly from your wallet.

Advantages of DEX

  • Full control of your funds
  • No KYC requirements
  • Censorship-resistant
  • Access to all listed tokens (including new ones)
  • No counterparty risk
  • True cryptocurrency principles

Disadvantages of DEX

  • Requires technical knowledge (wallet setup)
  • Lower liquidity (higher spreads)
  • Slower transactions (blockchain dependent)
  • Higher gas fees
  • Risk of losing access (if you lose private keys)

Popular DEX Platforms

  • Uniswap: Largest DEX, Ethereum
  • PancakeSwap: Binance Smart Chain
  • Raydium: Solana ecosystem
  • Curve: Specialized for stablecoins

Account Setup & KYC

Most CEX require Know Your Customer (KYC) verification for regulatory compliance.

KYC Process

  1. Create account with email and password
  2. Verify email address
  3. Provide personal information
  4. Upload identity documents (passport, ID)
  5. Sometimes facial recognition verification
  6. Approval usually within 24 hours

Privacy Considerations

  • KYC creates identity link to your holdings
  • Data breaches expose personal info
  • Governments can subpoena records
  • For privacy, use DEX (but with risks)

Trading Pairs

A trading pair is two cryptocurrencies you're trading between, like BTC/USD or ETH/BTC.

Reading Trading Pairs

  • BTC/USD: Buy Bitcoin with US Dollars
  • ETH/BTC: Buy Ethereum with Bitcoin
  • USDC/ETH: Buy USDC stablecoin with Ethereum
  • First symbol = what you're buying (base)
  • Second symbol = what you're using (quote)

Order Types

Market Order

  • Buy/sell immediately at current price
  • Guaranteed execution
  • Price may be slightly worse than displayed
  • Good for quick trades

Limit Order

  • Set your desired buy/sell price
  • Only executes if price reaches your limit
  • May not execute if price never reached
  • Better price control

Stop-Loss Order

  • Set trigger price to sell automatically
  • Protects against major losses
  • Executes as market order when triggered
  • Essential risk management tool

Fees

  • Trading Fee: Percentage of trade value (0.1-0.25% typical)
  • Withdrawal Fee: Cost to move crypto off exchange
  • Deposit Fee: Usually free, but depends on method
  • Maker vs Taker: Different fees for placing vs filling orders

Exchange Security

Exchanges are frequent targets for hackers. Protect yourself:

  • Use strong, unique passwords
  • Enable 2FA (authenticator app, not SMS if possible)
  • Whitelist withdrawal addresses
  • Don't keep large amounts on exchange
  • Use reputable, regulated exchanges
  • Verify withdrawal addresses before sending
