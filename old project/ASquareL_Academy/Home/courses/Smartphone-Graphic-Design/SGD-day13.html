DAY 13: LOGO DESIGN FOUNDATIONS

Master the art of creating memorable logos that endure across decades

Logo Principles

A logo is a brand's visual signature. It must work at all scales (favicon to billboard). It must work in single color (black/white). It must be memorable. It must age well. It must work on mobile especially—most first encounters happen on smartphone screens.

Mark vs. Wordmark

Mark: Icon-based (Nike swoosh). Wordmark: Text-based (Google). Combo: Both together. Different applications suit different types. Understand nuances. Choose strategically.

LOGO RULE: Test your logo as a favicon (16x16px). If it's unrecognizable at that size, it's too complex. Simplicity is sophistication.

Logo Grid Systems

Professional logos use grid systems. Circles, squares, triangles—geometric foundations. This creates mathematical harmony. It ensures consistency. It makes refinement easier. Start with grid. Build on grid. Your logo appears intentional and balanced.

Color and Logo

Design logo first in black/white. Then add color. The best logos work in single color. Color is secondary, not primary. Reverse this in your process and your logo will be weak.

Negative Space Logos

The space around your logo matters as much as the logo itself. Use negative space strategically. Create hidden imagery. Amazon logo: smile between A and Z. The negative space adds dimension without complexity.

Logo Variations

Create logo in multiple variations: full horizontal, stacked, icon-only, with tagline, without. Each variation serves different applications. A flexible logo system scales infinitely across applications.
