DAY 11: ACCESSIBILITY IN DESIGN

Create inclusive designs that serve all users with dignity and precision

WCAG Standards: The Framework

Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) define accessibility standards. AA level is industry standard. AAA is premium accessibility. For text, you need 4.5:1 contrast ratio (AA) or 7:1 (AAA). Large text needs 3:1 (AA) or 4.5:1 (AAA).

Contrast Ratio Calculation

Use accessibility checkers to verify contrast. Don't guess. Chrome DevTools has built-in checker. WebAIM has free online tools. Contrast is non-negotiable. Poor contrast creates barriers for millions of users.

ACCESSIBILITY RULE: If you wouldn't read your design in the sun on your phone, your contrast is too low. Test in bright daylight. Real-world use is your quality bar.

Color Blindness Considerations

8% of males, 0.5% of females have color blindness. Protanopia (red-blind) and Deuteranopia (green-blind) are most common. Never rely on color alone to communicate information. Use patterns, icons, or text labels alongside color.

Accessible Color Palettes

Use tools like Coblis to simulate color blindness. Design your palette. Check through three color blindness filters. If your design works for color-blind users, it works for everyone.

Font and Size Accessibility

Minimum font size for body text: 14px on mobile. For older users, 16px+. Line height of 1.5x minimum. Adequate spacing between letters prevents letter confusion. These aren't style choices—they're accessibility requirements.

Typography Accessibility

Avoid thin fonts at small sizes. Avoid all-caps text for body content (affects screen reader readability). Avoid decorative fonts for content. Use readable typefaces. Accessibility and readability are inseparable concepts.

The Business Case for Accessibility

Accessible design reaches more people. It builds trust. It's often legal requirement. Clients want accessibility. Your competitive advantage: Designs that work for everyone are simply better designs.
