DAY 7: ADVANCED TYPOGRAPHY TECHNIQUES

Elevate your type game: kerning, leading, hierarchy, and expressive typographic systems

Kerning: The Space Between Letters

Kerning isn't decoration. It's precision engineering. A poorly kerned headline reads as unprofessional, even if the typeface is beautiful. Perfect kerning feels invisible—viewers don't notice it, but they feel its quality.

Manual vs. Automatic Kerning

Auto-kerning (optical) handles 80% of cases well. Manual kerning perfects the remaining 20%. When scaling designs for mobile, kerning distances scale with your font size. This relationship changes at different scales.

KERNING RULE: Kern headlines manually. Small body text doesn't need manual kerning. Focus effort on elements viewers stare at longest.

Leading: The Vertical Rhythm

Leading (line height) determines readability more than font choice. Too tight: eye confusion. Too loose: disjointed. Perfect leading feels like natural breathing rhythm. For body text on mobile, aim for 1.5x to 1.8x line height.

Paragraph Spacing

Space between paragraphs needs more than default line height. Use 1.5-2x line height between paragraphs. This creates visual grouping without adding layout complexity.

Typography Hierarchy in Action

H1 (headline): 32-48px. H2 (section): 24-28px. H3 (subsection): 18-20px. Body: 14-16px. Captions: 12px. These sizes create visual hierarchy on mobile. Maintain this progression consistently across all designs.

Weight Variation

Don't just change size. Vary weight. Bold headlines, regular body, light captions. Weight creates hierarchy as powerfully as size does.

HIERARCHY TIP: Test hierarchy by converting your design to grayscale. Can viewers still identify hierarchy? If not, it's too subtle. Hierarchy should survive color removal.

Optical Adjustment

Letter metrics don't account for letter shape. The letter 'V' at 100px looks smaller than 'O' at 100px. Adjust font size optically. Trust your eyes more than pixel measurements.
